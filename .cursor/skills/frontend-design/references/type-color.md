# Type and color from the subject

Do not pick a "nice pairing" from memory. Derive both from the brief's materials, then commit.

When the pairing is the problem, continue in [typography.md](typography.md). When the palette is the problem, continue in [color.md](color.md). Direction (what the pair must serve) lives in [direction.md](direction.md).

## Type method

1. Name the voice in one adjective that belongs to this subject (still, clerical, coastal, industrial, liturgical, dry). Not "modern" or "premium."
2. Choose a display face that can say that adjective at large size without costume. Then a body face that can carry paragraphs at 16–18px without competing.
3. Write why this pair is not interchangeable with the last site you designed. If you cannot, you are in a default.
4. Set a scale: display, h1, h2, kicker, body, caption, button. Kickers are small and tracked only if the voice is archival or industrial; wellness and pastoral voices usually need less tracking, not more.
5. Variable axes are a tool, not a personality. SOFT, WONK, opsz, and extreme optical size read as character in a quiet interior and as noise on a photograph. On a living hero plate, prefer a calm cut of the same family (or the body sans at display size) so the plate can act.

After the pair is chosen, set a paragraph and squint: the body must hold as a rectangle of ink (typographic color). If it looks spindly or like a slab, fix cut/weight/leading before you darken the hex. Full setting rules: [typography.md](typography.md).

Fraunces + a grotesque can be right for a reflective practice. The same pair with full wonk on a sea video will look like a poster stuck on a window. Turn the axis down; keep Fraunces for section titles over paper.

## What type should not do

- Do not use the same three Google pairings on every brief (Newsreader + something cream, Inter everywhere, "editorial serif + neon mono").
- Do not letter-space body copy. Tracking belongs to kickers and sometimes all-caps identity, never paragraphs.
- Do not make the H1 a different alignment, color, and motion language from the rest of the identity. The wordmark and the H1 are relatives.
- Line length for body: roughly 60–72 characters. Full-bleed paragraphs are not "open," they are unread.

## Color method

1. Find a physical material in the brief: the water in the work, the paper on the desk, the cloth, the metal, the hour of day. If you have a photograph, sample it. If you do not, describe the material first, then pick hex.
2. Build a short token list:
   - `--paper` — page ground
   - `--paper-2` — tinted alternate chapter
   - `--surface` — cards, raised sheets
   - `--ink` — text
   - `--deep` — footer / dark band
   - `--accent` — one working color, used rarely
3. Name tokens after roles, not after hues (`--water`, `--ink`, `--paper`). Hue names go stale the moment the palette shifts.
4. Contrast is a requirement. Ink on paper must read; white type on a photograph must read without crushing the photograph. Prefer text-shadow / local contrast over a full-image overlay.

## Palettes that are not choices

Unless the brief asks for them:

- Warm cream (~#F4F1EA) + terracotta + high-contrast serif
- Near-black + acid green or vermilion
- Gold / navy "finance"
- Fog-sage wellness green on stone, used as a generic calm
- Gradient mesh blobs as brand color

A blue-hour still-water palette and a fog-stone palette can both be right for reflective work. The difference is whether you sampled the actual plate (dusk sea vs forest moss). Sample, then name.

## Accent discipline

One accent. Buttons, links, and focus can share it. A second accent is a second brand. Featured cards may fill with the deep or the accent — not with a new hue.

Hover and focus states shift shadow and lift, not hue, unless the brief's identity is chromatic by nature (a gallery, a festival).

## Dark and light chapters

Dark chapters use the deep token, not a random charcoal. Light chapters use paper / surface. Do not invent a third neutral family for "the about band." If a section feels like it needs a new color, it usually needs a new amount of air.
