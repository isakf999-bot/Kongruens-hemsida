# Direction: inventing a look that belongs here

Read this when the brief is thin, when you would otherwise "pick a nice palette," or when two directions feel equally pretty. Pretty is not a direction.

## The job of a direction

A direction is a set of refusals plus one risk. Without refusals, every page becomes a moodboard. Without a risk, every page becomes a template with better fonts.

Write the direction in six lines before tokens:

1. **Subject** — the actual thing (a reed lake at blue hour, a machine shop, a parish newsletter), not the category ("wellness," "SaaS").
2. **Audience + job** — who is here, what one action or understanding this page is for.
3. **Material** — the physical stuff you will sample (water, paper, wool, oxide, fluorescent tube). One sentence.
4. **Voice adjective** — one word that is not "modern," "premium," "clean," or "minimal."
5. **Signature** — the one thing the page will be remembered by. Medium: type, plate, motion, object, or interaction — pick one.
6. **Refusals** — three treatments you will not use even if they would look "finished" (cream wash, overlapping hero sheet, acid accent, numbered pillars, etc.).

If line 5 and line 6 contradict, the signature is not a signature. It is decoration you are afraid to commit to.

## Where distinctive choices come from

Not from other websites in the same industry. Industry sites have already collapsed into a house style. Distinctive choices come from:

- The client's own artifacts: a handwritten note, a jacket, a boat, a tool, a room at a certain hour
- The weather and geography of the work, not a stock "nature" folder
- The vernacular of the field (a tide table, a hymn board, a packing slip) used as structure, not as clipart
- A constraint in the brief (locked copy, no video, must be Swedish, must book by mail)

If you have none of that, invent a specific room and design for that room. "A practice" is not a room. "A still lake just after the sun has gone, seen wide, with type sitting in the air above the water" is a room.

## Two directions, then one

Always sketch two. They must disagree about something load-bearing (centered vs rail lockup, still vs living plate, serif display vs sans display, paper-light vs deep-dark interior). If they only disagree about accent hue, you sketched one direction twice.

Pick by asking: which one could not be reused on a different subject next week? Keep that one. Say out loud what you threw away.

## The signature inventory

Choose one. Everything else supports it.

| Signature | What it demands | What it forbids |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Living plate (video / weather) | Quiet type, no overlay, no second motion | Ken Burns on other routes, gray wash |
| A display cut used with nerve | Calm plate, generous measure, few weights | Wonk *and* a busy photo |
| An object or product in space | Real lighting, real materials, time to model or photograph | Mesh-gradient atmosphere behind it |
| A structural idea (chapters, a ledger, a sequence) | The content must actually be that structure | Fake 01/02/03 on unrelated cards |
| An interaction (configurator, map, instrument) | It has to work; the rest of the page stays still | Extra hover theater on static cards |

If you cannot fill the "forbids" column, you have not chosen yet.

## Spending taste without copying a genre

Pastoral, editorial, industrial, liturgical, coastal — these are climates, not kits. A coastal site is not obligatory stripes and rope. It is horizon, weather, and a type size that can stand in wind. An editorial site is not obligatory hairlines; it is measure, crop, and a display face that can hold a headline.

When you feel yourself reaching for genre props (compass icons, leaves, circuit traces, marble), stop. Ask what material those props were standing in for, and use the material instead.

## Locked briefs

If copy, prices, names, or typos are locked, they are part of the material. Design the lockup for the real string lengths. Do not write a prettier headline in the plan and then "also support" the real one. The real one is the headline.

If the client has rejected a look, that rejection is a refusal. Write it into line 6. Do not smuggle it back as a "refined" version of the same palette.

## When you are stuck

You are usually stuck in one of three places:

1. **No material** — you are mixing hex from adjectives. Go get or describe a photograph.
2. **Two signatures** — video *and* theatrical type *and* overlapping card. Delete until one remains.
3. **Fear of empty** — you are adding a line, a frame, a wash, a fourth color. Add air or change type size instead.

Do not add a component. Change a relationship (type to plate, rail to lockup, paper to deep).
