# Critique: looking until it is true

Read this before you call a pass done, and whenever something feels "almost." Almost is a specific kind of wrong: the plan was right and the last 10% is generic chrome, mis-measure, or a second signature.

## Pass order (do not skip)

Change one layer at a time. If you fix color and type and overlay in the same hour, you will not know what worked.

1. **Job + signature** — Does the page still keep the six-line direction? If the signature is the sea, and you added a serif stunt and a floating card, stop and delete.
2. **Composition** — Squint. One dominant? Lockup a group? Rail shared with identity? Chapter turn after the hero?
3. **Type** — Does the pair still belong to the subject at actual sizes? Display fighting the plate? Body measure readable? Kickers over-tracked?
4. **Color** — Paper/ink/deep from the material? One accent? Type on the plate readable without a wash?
5. **Chrome** — Buttons, cards, lines, shadows. Anything that could be removed without losing meaning?
6. **Motion** — One choreography? Reduced-motion path? Hover as confirmation, not a second show?
7. **States + small screens** — Focus visible, forms usable, 390px lockup still a group.

If step 2 fails, do not kern the H1.

## Questions that fail a pass

Ask them as a stranger, not as the author.

- Could I reuse this palette and pairing on a dentist, a fund, and a candle brand by changing the photo? If yes, you designed a theme, not a subject.
- What is the first thing I remember with my eyes closed? If it is "a rounded white card" or "a navy button," the signature lost.
- Where did I get nervous about empty and add something? Remove that addition and look again.
- Does the photograph still look like a photograph, or like a background texture? Wash, blur, and multiply are how photos become textures.
- Is the nav and the H1 the same family of decisions (color, rail, case, weight), or did the hero start a new brand?
- Would a careful human have measured this, or only typed `padding-left: 4rem` in two different wrappers?

## One variable

When iterating with a user, change one relationship per pass when you can: lockup vertical placement *or* type cut *or* button size. Bundling five tweaks teaches neither of you what mattered, and reintroduces tells.

Name the variable in the work ("same rail, optical center, quieter display") so the next pass has a memory.

## Optical QA (not a screenshot of the whole page)

A full-page shot hides almost. Crop to:

- Nav brand left edge vs H1 left edge (same viewport width)
- Lockup as a silhouette (include a little plate around it)
- One card at rest and one hovered
- The chapter join: last 80px of hero + first 80px of intro (this is where collage and dark-on-dark live)
- Mobile lockup including actions

Look at those crops at 1x, not only as a tiny thumbnail.

## The "finished" tells

Work looks finished and generic when:

- Every corner has the same radius
- Every image has the same ratio
- Every section has the same padding and a heading of the same size
- Shadows are the browser default
- The accent is used on every icon, every kicker, and the button

Finish is variation inside a system: same tokens, different measures, one featured exception.

## Chanel, operationalized

Remove accessories in this order (stop when the page gets worse, put that one back):

1. Extra overlay / filter / scrim
2. Extra line, frame, or hairline
3. Extra accent hue
4. Extra motion (second zoom, hover scale on images)
5. Extra type stunt (wonk, giant italic, outlined headline)
6. Extra component (the overlapping sheet, the stats row, the logo cloud)

The signature should be the thing you cannot remove. If you can remove everything and the page is still "a nice layout," you never chose.

## Talking about taste

Prefer physical sentences: "the title is sitting in the reeds," "the button is louder than the horizon," "the intro is a second hero." Avoid "make it pop," "more premium," "more wow" as instructions to yourself — translate those into a variable (contrast, weight, crop, signature medium).

If a user uses those words, translate with them once ("wow here = living plate, not animation on the cards"), then execute the variable.
